Session 01 — So… What exactly is AI?

AI = computer systems that do tasks needing some human-like intelligence: spotting patterns, understanding language, predicting, generating content.

AI Vocabulary Starter Pack

AI

Artificial Intelligence
Machines doing tasks that normally need human-like thinking.

ML

Machine Learning
Computers learning patterns from data instead of only fixed rules.

LLM

Large Language Model
AI trained on huge amounts of text to understand and write language.

NLP

Natural Language Processing
Helping computers read, understand and use human language.

AI tools we already knew

💬 ChatGPT

Text assistant for answers, brainstorming, writing, learning.

✨ Gemini

Google’s assistant; works with text and other kinds of information.

🧠 Claude

Anthropic’s assistant for reasoning, writing, analysis.

🎨 Canva

Design platform with AI-powered tools for creating content.

Session 03 — Just because AI can… should it?

Ethical vs unethical use. Tap the scale to tilt it.

✅ Ethical AI

• Label AI-generated content
• Assist, don’t deceive
• Respect privacy
• Ask consent
• Fact-check output
• Avoid harmful stereotypes

🚫 Unethical AI

• Deepfakes that deceive
• Fake ads & misleading product images
• Impersonating people
• Passing AI content off as real
• Using identity without consent

When AI content goes wrong

🍔 1 · Listing vs delivery

Glossy, towering and perfectly lit online. In real life: squished and smaller. Polished or AI-made photos can oversell.

👗 2 · Flipkart: ordered vs received

Session 04 — What kind of AI user are you?

Consumer→Operator→Architect

1 · Consumers

Use ready-made AI tools.
e.g. ChatGPT to summarise notes.

2 · Operators

Guide and combine tools to finish tasks and workflows.
e.g. Research → create → refine a project with several AIs.

3 · Architects

Design AI systems, agents or solutions.
e.g. An AI-powered app or automated workflow.

5 levels of AI thinking

L1 AskSimple answers
L2 PromptBetter instructions + context
L3 EvaluateQuestion, check, improve
L4 IntegrateMix AI with workflows
L5 CreateDesign AI solutions
📝 Not an official academic model, just a practical classroom framework.

Session 11 — Recreate the image 🖼

We were shown 3–4 reference images and had to prompt an AI to recreate each as closely as possible. Here are two I made, with prompts that could generate something very similar:

How to recreate an identical image

• Observe first: list every detail you see
• Name the subject and exact counts (2 scoops, 4 rings)
• Pose, framing, camera angle (low angle, close-up)
• Lighting: harsh sun or soft studio, where shadows fall
• Exact colours, not just “blue”
• Background and textures (wallpaper print, waffle cone)
• Style and mood: “editorial fashion photo”, “bright summer ad”
• Compare side by side, then change one thing at a time
• Say what to avoid (extra fingers, random text)

GOOD IMAGE PROMPT=Subject+Details+Composition+Lighting+Style+Mood
The AI only sees my words, so better observation = better prompting.

Session 13 — LLM vs AI Agent vs Agentic AI

Some systems mainly generate responses, some use tools to finish tasks, and more agentic ones coordinate many steps toward a goal.

LLMAI AgentAgentic AI
IdeaLanguage modelGoal-oriented systemSystem/workflow with more autonomy across steps
RoleUnderstand & generate languageComplete tasks with reasoning/toolsPlan, adapt, run multi-step workflows
ToolsNot inherently neededOften uses tools/APIsMay coordinate many tools/agents
AutonomyLow aloneMediumHigher
ExampleAnswering a questionBooking/search assistantPlans, researches, acts, adjusts
These terms are still evolving, so definitions overlap.

AGI — Artificial General Intelligence

A hypothetical AI that can handle a broad range of intellectual tasks at a human-like level instead of narrow ones. Today’s common AI tools aren’t generally considered AGI.

My Reflection

When I started Fundamentals of AI, I thought AI was basically a faster Google. Session by session, that changed. I learned what AI really is, and I tried one prompt on ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude and saw that the same prompt doesn’t give the same answer.

I learned to doubt my own eyes in AI or Real, and to ask “should it?” in the ethics session, from misleading food and outfit photos to AI creators who never say they’re AI. The user types, the glossary and the family tree gave me words and a map, and classification versus prediction showed me how everyday apps “think”.

Joan Is Awful scared me a little: consent, identity and who owns “you”. Then it got fun. I personalised ChatGPT, explored worlds with World Labs, recreated images with careful prompts and built a web app with Emergent without writing code. LLMs, agents and agentic AI showed me how fast this field moves.

The biggest lesson wasn’t what AI can do. It was how thoughtfully I should use it. I came in as a user. I’m leaving as someone who questions, checks and creates.
